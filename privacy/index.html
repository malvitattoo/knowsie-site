This policy describes the Knowsie app for iPhone and Android, version 1.3.0, including the updates we deliver inside the app. Until a new version of the app renames it, your phone may still show its earlier name, Malvi.

Knowsie (“we”) is an app that scans packaged food, reads the ingredients and rates them. This policy explains what we collect, why, who processes it, and the choices you have. We don’t show ads, we don’t track you across other companies’ apps or websites, and we don’t sell your data.

Recipe changes and notifications

Recipe changes. Recipe changes and favourites need an account. For the products in your History that you’ve scanned or opened in the last 6 months, and for your favourites (up to 200 products per account, favourites first), our server checks Open Food Facts again, usually once a day. It sends only the barcodes, never anything about you or about who has the product. When a product’s ingredients or allergen information change, we record that the change is for your account, and when you saw it in the app. These records are deleted 6 months after we find the change, within a day once the product is in neither your History nor your favourites, or with your account. What a change means for you, given your allergies, typed allergens, diets, goals and watch list, is worked out on your phone; our servers don’t use your preferences for this. Your phone keeps the recipe changes listed for you, and what each one means for you, until you sign out.

To spot changes, our server keeps the latest Open Food Facts version of each product it checks, and the changes it finds. That’s product information, with nothing about who has the product. A product’s version is deleted once we stop checking it (and at least 7 days after we first saved it), and the changes 6 months after we find them.

Notifications (not available yet). The app doesn’t offer push notifications yet, so for now you’ll find recipe changes only in the app. When we add them, they’ll work like this, and we’ll update this policy first if anything changes:

  • Knowsie will send them only if you turn them on in the app and allow them when your phone asks. You’ll be able to turn them off at any time in Profile → Preferences or in your phone’s settings.
  • Their text will always be one of two short messages: “A product from your history or favourites changed its recipe.” or, for two or more products, “Some products from your history or favourites changed their recipes.” They’ll never name a product, ingredient, allergen, diet or score, and you’ll get at most one a day.
  • While they’re on, we’ll store your phone’s push token (an Expo push token), its platform, the app version and when the app last ran on it while you were signed in, linked to your account, and we’ll note which recipe changes a notification told you about. We’ll delete the token when you turn notifications off in the app (if you do it in your phone’s settings, the next time the app starts while you’re signed in), sign out on that phone or delete your account, when Expo tells us it no longer works (for example, after you delete the app), or 180 days after the app last ran there while you were signed in. If you turn them off or sign out while you’re offline, the app tells us the next time it starts with an account signed in. For each notification we send, we’ll keep Expo’s ticket number with the token until we get Expo’s receipt for it, for at most 2 days.
  • To create the token, the app will send Expo the push address Apple gives your phone, a random ID for this installation and the app’s ID. Notifications will go through Expo and Apple Push Notification service. They’ll start on iPhone; Android isn’t supported yet.
  • If you sign out, that phone stops getting notifications. Your choice is remembered on the phone and applies again if you sign back in with the same account.

How we use it

  • To score products. The base score is the same for everyone. If you choose nutrition goals, they can move your score up or down by up to 15 points. Your allergies, typed allergens and diets add warnings, and the alternatives we suggest leave out products whose information conflicts with them. Your watch list adds alerts. None of these four change the score.
  • To keep your scan history and favourites in your account when you’re signed in, and your preferences too if you agree, so they’re there on your other devices.
  • To check the products in your History and your favourites for recipe changes, and show you in the app what changed and what it means for you.
  • To show you badges for being curious and for helping: scanning, reading about ingredients, adding products and reporting problems. They never depend on what you eat, and no one else using Knowsie can see them. When you tap Share on a badge, your phone gets a short text with a link to knowsie.app; we don’t see what you share or with whom.
  • To read the label photos you take and fill in the product’s details for you to check.
  • To review products and reports you send in, and add them, or the corrections, to our product database, where every user can see them. A front photo you send, or a photo from a problem report, may become the product’s picture.
  • To read your feedback and your emails, and answer them.
  • To find and fix bugs and keep the app stable, using crash, error and stability reports, if you turned them on.
  • To run your account and, later, your subscription.
  • To grow our ingredient list from the ingredient names we don’t recognize.

We don’t use your data for advertising, and we don’t sell it.

Legal bases

Data protection laws, such as the GDPR in the EU and the UK, ask us to name the legal reason (the “legal basis”) for each use of your data. We rely on four:

  • Contract: we need the data to give you a feature you asked for (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR).
  • Consent: you said yes, and you can take it back at any time (Article 6(1)(a)). For health information and beliefs, it’s your explicit consent (Article 9(2)(a)).
  • Legitimate interests: we have a good reason that doesn’t outweigh your rights, such as keeping Knowsie secure or the product database accurate (Article 6(1)(f)). You can object to these uses (see Your choices and rights).
  • Legal obligation: a law requires us to keep the data (Article 6(1)(c)).

Where they are, and how transfers are protected

Some of these services work for us and use your data only on our instructions (“processors”). Others decide for themselves how they use what they receive, under their own privacy policies (“independent controllers”): Open Food Facts; Apple and Google, when you sign in with them, use their stores or, on Android, Google’s barcode reader; Expo, for update checks; and GitHub and jsDelivr, for visits to our website.

When data from the EU, the UK or Switzerland goes to the United States, it’s protected in one of two ways. Either the company is certified under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework, with its UK Extension and the Swiss-U.S. Data Privacy Framework, or its agreement includes the European Commission’s standard contractual clauses, with the UK and Swiss additions. The Data Privacy Framework covers only the United States, so where Google keeps data in other countries, standard contractual clauses protect it there. Data that goes to the UK needs neither: the EU and Switzerland recognise that the UK protects data well enough. We checked each US company against the official Data Privacy Framework list at dataprivacyframework.gov on 27 October 2026.

Health information

Allergies, typed allergens and nutrition goals can count as health information. Halal and kosher diets can reveal religious beliefs, and vegan or vegetarian diets can reveal other beliefs. We use them only to personalize the app: your warnings, the effect of your goals on your scores, the alternatives we suggest, and what a recipe change means for you (worked out on your phone). We never use them for advertising, and we never sell them.

They’re optional. You can choose them when you first open the app or later. You can leave them empty, change them or clear them at any time in Profile → Preferences. If you’re not signed in, they stay on your phone.

Your consent. If you’re signed in, we save them in your account only if you agree. The app asks you once: when you first choose them while signed in, when you sign in with preferences already on your phone, or, if you already had an account, once after this update. The question names these kinds of data and what we use them for, and the box isn’t ticked for you. We keep a record of your answer in your account: when you agreed, when you withdrew if you do, and which version of the question you saw. If you don’t agree, your preferences stay on your phone only, we remove any copy already in your account, and the app keeps working with them on your phone. You can change your answer, or withdraw your consent, at any time in Profile → Preferences (see Your choices and rights).

Older versions of the app. The question comes in an app update. Your phone downloads it while the app is open, and it starts working when you tap Restart on “A new version is ready”, or the next time the app starts. Until then, if you’re signed in, the app keeps saving your preferences in your account without asking, as before. Versions before 1.0.15 can’t receive the update at all, so if you’re signed in on one, it keeps doing this. Please update the app.

Not medical advice. Knowsie gives general information about food ingredients. It isn’t medical advice, and it doesn’t replace a doctor, dietitian or pharmacist. Your preferences help the app point things out, but product and allergen details come from labels, Open Food Facts and people who send in products, so they can be incomplete, out of date or wrong, and recipes change. Always read the package, and if you have an allergy or a health condition, follow your doctor’s advice.

How long we keep it

  • Account, preferences, scan history and favourites: until you delete your account (a favourite also goes when you remove its heart). Deleting your account in the app removes them from our servers right away, together with your recipe change records. If you ask us by email instead, we delete them within 7 days. Our current database plan doesn’t include backups, so we don’t keep a backup copy.
  • Recipe change records: 6 months after we find the change, within a day once the product is in neither your History nor your favourites, or with your account.
  • Badges and badge counters: until you delete your account. On your phone: a guest’s badges until you delete the app; a signed-in copy until you sign out or delete your account in the app.
  • Favourites and recipe changes on your phone: until you sign out or delete your account in the app. If your sign-in ends some other way, for example because you deleted your account on another phone, they stay on that phone until you delete the app there, or sign in there again with the same account and then sign out.
  • Sign-in records (IP address and device type): until you sign out on that device or delete your account. Deleting the app without signing out doesn’t end them.
  • Preferences on your phone: if you’re not signed in, until you clear them or delete the app. If you’re signed in, until you sign out or delete your account in the app. If your sign-in ends some other way, for example because you deleted your account on another phone, they stay on that phone until you sign out there or delete the app.
  • Products and problem reports you send in, with their photos: kept while we review them. Approved ones become part of our product database and stay there. Ones we reject are deleted, with their photos, 90 days after we reject them.
  • If we don’t add a product you sent us, or don’t change a product after your report, we keep the reason with your submission (one choice from a short list, or a short note from our team) and delete it together with the submission 90 days after we reviewed it. Approved submissions are kept as product data, as before.
  • Feedback, with its screenshot: deleted 12 months after you send it. A screenshot whose message never reached us is deleted after 7 days.
  • Your answer to saving your preferences in your account (when you agreed, when you withdrew if you do, and which version of the question you saw): until you delete your account.
  • Emails you send us, and our replies: deleted about 2 years after the conversation ends. Every 3 months, we delete the conversations that ended more than 2 years ago and empty the mailbox’s trash. Ask us and we’ll delete yours sooner.
  • Password reset emails: our mailbox keeps a copy of each one we send. Every 3 months, we delete these copies and empty the mailbox’s trash, so each copy is kept for about 3 months. Ask us and we’ll delete yours sooner. The link in it works only once, for a limited time.
  • Server request logs: 1 day on our current plan.
  • Crash and error reports, if you turned them on: 30 days. Turning crash reports off stops new ones; the ones already sent are deleted when their 30 days are up.
  • Subscription records (iPhone only): kept by RevenueCat for as long as Knowsie uses RevenueCat, unless we delete them sooner. Write to us and we’ll have yours deleted within 7 days. Once subscriptions start, records of sales that tax and accounting rules require us to keep are kept for as long as those rules require.
  • Update checks: handled by Expo under its own privacy policy. They don’t include your account.
  • Label photos read by Anthropic: deleted by Anthropic within 30 days, except in the cases described under “Who processes it”.
  • Read-outs of your label photos (the details Anthropic read from the label, never the photos themselves, and nothing that links them to your account, IP address or device): kept on our server for 15 minutes so the app can ask again if the answer didn’t reach your phone, and deleted within 30 minutes.

Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example the EU or the UK), some or all of these may be legal rights. We offer them to everyone. You can:

  • ask what data we have about you, and get a copy (access);
  • ask us to correct data that’s wrong (rectification);
  • ask us to delete your data (erasure);
  • ask us to stop using your data for a while and only keep it, for example while we check whether it’s correct (restriction);
  • ask for the data you gave us in a common format you can take elsewhere (portability);
  • object to the uses that rely on our legitimate interests (objection);
  • withdraw your consent at any time, wherever we rely on it. That doesn’t change how we used your data before.

Write to privacy@knowsie.app. We may ask you to confirm it’s you, usually by writing from your account’s email address. We answer without undue delay, and within a month. If a request is complex, or you send us several, we may need up to two more months; if so, we’ll tell you why within the first month.

In the EU and the UK, you can also complain to a data protection authority, for example where you live or work. In Poland, that’s the UODO; in the UK, the ICO.

No automated decisions about you. Scores, warnings and the alternatives we suggest are worked out automatically, from the label and your preferences. They’re information to help you choose food, not decisions about you with legal or similarly significant effects (Article 22 GDPR).

We don’t sell your personal information, and we don’t share it for advertising, in the sense California law uses those words.

If you live in Washington State or Nevada

Washington’s My Health My Data Act and Nevada’s consumer health data law protect information about your health. In Knowsie, that can include your allergies, typed allergens, diets, nutrition goals and watch list. Whether or not these laws apply to Knowsie, we give you the rights they describe. You can:

  • ask whether we collect, share or sell this data, and see it, with a list of anyone we’ve shared it with and how to contact them. We don’t sell it, and we don’t share it: it’s stored in your account by Supabase, which works for us as our database provider, and only if you agreed (older versions of the app don’t ask; see Health information);
  • withdraw your consent, so we stop collecting it in your account (see Health information);
  • ask us to delete it. We delete it from our systems and tell our service providers to delete it too. Our current database plan doesn’t include backups, so there’s no backup copy to wait for.

Write to privacy@knowsie.app. You don’t need a Knowsie account to ask. Requests are free, up to twice a year. We answer within 45 days. If we need more time, we may take up to 45 more days, and we’ll tell you why within the first 45. If we turn down your request, you can appeal: reply to our answer with “Appeal” in the subject. We’ll tell you in writing what we decided, and why, within 45 days. If we turn down your appeal, we’ll tell you how to contact your state’s Attorney General.

Children, security and international transfers

Children. Knowsie isn’t meant for children under 16, and we don’t knowingly collect their data. If you think a child has given us data, write to us and we’ll delete it.

Security. Data travels encrypted between the app and our services. On your phone, your sign-in is kept in the app’s own storage, which other apps can’t read. Apart from the AI service that reads label photos (see “Who processes it”), only our team can open the photos you send before they’re approved. If we approve a product and use one of its photos as the product’s picture, that photo becomes public: anyone with its web address can see it. No system is completely secure, so we can’t guarantee it.

International transfers. Supabase, RevenueCat and Sentry keep the data they receive from Knowsie in the United States. Anthropic, Expo, Apple, Google and GitHub (which hosts our website) are US companies, and may process data there too. Open Food Facts is in France, and jsDelivr, which delivers code for our password reset page, is in the UK. How each transfer from the EU, the UK and Switzerland is protected is listed under Where they are.
